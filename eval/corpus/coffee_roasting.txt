Coffee roasting transforms green beans into the aromatic brown beans used for brewing. The process has three main stages: drying, browning, and development. During drying, moisture is driven off at gentle heat. The browning stage triggers the Maillard reaction, creating hundreds of flavor compounds. Development, the final stage after first crack, determines the roast level: light roasts retain more acidity and origin character, while dark roasts taste smokier and more bitter because prolonged heat breaks down sugars and oils. Cooling the beans quickly after roasting locks in the desired flavor profile.
