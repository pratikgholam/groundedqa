Lake Voss is a closed-basin lake in the southern highlands of Mars, fed only by seasonal meltwater from the surrounding scarps. Because it has no outlet, minerals carried in by runoff accumulate over time, leaving the water increasingly saline. Orbital spectra show magnesium sulfate and chloride deposits ringing the shoreline, evidence of repeated wet-dry cycles. Planetary scientists consider the basin a promising site to search for preserved biosignatures, since salty, intermittently wet environments can shelter microbial life. Any future lander would target the delta deposits where ancient inflow channels entered the lake.
