Roman aqueducts carried water from distant springs and rivers into cities using a gentle, constant downward slope. Most of the channel ran underground or at ground level, with the famous arched bridges used only to cross valleys where the gradient demanded it. Water flowed by gravity alone, with no pumps, and a drop of only a few inches per hundred feet was enough to keep it moving over long distances. At the city, water entered a distribution tank called a castellum, which split the supply among public fountains, baths, and private customers. The aqueducts were a major factor in the growth and sanitation of ancient Rome.
