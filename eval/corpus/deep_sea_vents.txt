Deep-sea hydrothermal vents form where seawater seeps into the ocean crust, is heated by magma, and gushes back out laden with dissolved minerals. When the hot fluid meets cold seawater, minerals precipitate into chimney-like structures called black smokers. These vents support ecosystems that do not rely on sunlight. Instead, chemosynthetic bacteria oxidize hydrogen sulfide to produce energy, forming the base of a food web that includes giant tube worms, clams, and vent crabs. The discovery of vent life changed assumptions about where life can exist, including on icy moons such as Europa.
